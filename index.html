      05 / 05

      Yellow Cells

      Workbook tools for school district offices.

      The problemDistrict offices rebuild the same state workbooks by hand every year, and nothing checks the numbers.

      District offices live in state and federal reporting spreadsheets. Yellow Cells rebuilds those workbooks as web tools that check their own numbers and open with a double-click. The bundle of tools inside it is called District Desk.

      What I built

      • 41 district office workbooks, most of them state and federal forms, rebuilt as web tools.
      • Every tool checks its own numbers: results turn green, amber or red, and a warning says exactly what is short and by how much.
      • The tools are plain pages with no server and no install: entries save in the browser, export to CSV and print as a clean report. The app around them handles sign-in, team accounts and payment.
      • A launcher with search and categories, from finance and tax to special education and food service.
      • A gate that runs every tool at desktop and phone width before anything ships.

      Process

      How I work

      How a job goes

      1. You tell me what the business is and what the tool needs to do.
      2. I answer with a fixed price and a date.
      3. Half up front, and the work starts.
      4. The software is yours. Hosting and upkeep are a monthly amount, quoted with the build.
      1. Agents draft and build. I decide what gets built, and nothing reaches production without my yes, each time.
      2. They work under written rules: no spending, no deploy without my yes, nothing sent without a cap in the code that sends.
      3. Every substantial build gets independent review rounds by agents that did not write it.
      4. A tester agent with no context walks each product the way a stranger would.
      5. I write down a guess before each build and the actual after. Of around seventy, about two thirds came in under, about a fifth over, the rest close.
